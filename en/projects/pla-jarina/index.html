PLA and jarina powder: development and characterization of a biocomposite.

01 / Research challenge

Why study this material?

This 2024–2025 PIBIC/PAIC project at UFAM, supervised by Prof. Marcionilo Neri da Silva Junior, investigated the feasibility of adding Amazonian jarina seed powder to a polylactic acid (PLA) matrix. The objective was to examine processing feasibility and the initial mechanical behavior of a natural-filler composite, without assuming that reinforcement would improve its properties.

02 / Methodology

From raw seed to test specimen

01
Seed preparation

28 seeds inspected (2 rejected), shell removal, fragmentation using a hydraulic press, knife milling and ring milling.

02
Sieving

100-mesh (149 µm) and 200-mesh (74 µm) screens; 102 g of selected powder obtained.

03
Compounding

MH100 homogenizer at 3,600 rpm; mixing trials with PLA, jarina powder and copaiba oil.

04
Molding

CAD/CAM design and machining of an ASTM D638 Type IV mold; small-scale injection molding.

05
Evaluation

Dimensional checks, tensile tests, and critical interpretation of stress–strain curves.

Engineering contributions and methods

This research involved hands-on work across jarina preparation, PLA processing and mechanical characterization. It connected tooling design with raw-material preparation and critical analysis of experimental limitations.

03 / Manufacturing and testing

Formulations and test process

Three formulations were prepared: PLA + copaiba oil, and the same matrix with 3% and 5% jarina powder. The report documents 17 mixing trials and 39 manufactured specimens (15 reference, 12 with 3%, and 12 with 5%). A reduced ASTM D638 Type IV mold was designed in SolidWorks, prepared for machining in NX 10 CAM and manufactured on a Romi D800 machining center. The specimens were intended for ASTM D638 tensile testing.

04 / Challenges

What went wrong — and why it matters

Challenging milling

Seed hardness required several processing stages and limited the fine-powder yield.

Brittle specimens

Unplasticized PLA mixtures crumbled; some molded samples had bubbles, uneven thickness, and premature fracture.

Thermal degradation

At 215 °C, copaiba-modified PLA degraded. At 190 °C, leakage and incomplete filling still affected molding.

Unreliable tensile data

Release agent contributed to slippage in the grips; several curves exhibited artifacts and some samples fractured during clamping.

05 / Findings

What the experiments actually showed

ProcessingPowder preparation

About 700 g of raw jarina seeds yielded 102 g of powder selected through processing and sieving.

MixingCopaiba oil effect

The plasticizer increased granule cohesion and apparent ductility during mixing, but reduced thermal stability during injection.

Experimental limitationTensile response

The measured 3% jarina specimen performed below the PLA reference specimens in the reported trials; sparse reliable data and grip slippage prevent a robust quantitative conclusion.

06 / Conclusions

Lessons and next steps

The study showed that jarina powder could be incorporated into PLA, but the available specimens and measurements did not establish an improvement in tensile strength. More importantly, the project identified practical barriers: filler–matrix compatibility, thermal control, injection quality, and test reliability. Proposed next steps include particle surface treatment, particle-size optimization, alternative natural plasticizers, and better controlled injection/extrusion.
