RCM control and visual servoing with Franka FR3.

Development and experimental evaluation of laparoscopic probe manipulation with a remote-center-of-motion constraint and RGB-D visual guidance.

Institut Pascal · SIGMA Clermont2026
01 / The challenge

The challenge

Robot-assisted laparoscopic manipulation requires the probe to rotate and translate around a fixed insertion point while a camera provides feedback for accurate placement.

02 / My work and methods

My work and methods

I implemented and evaluated Cartesian velocity and impedance control, a software-defined RCM task, RealSense eye-to-hand calibration and a five-feature IBVS loop. Task-priority control preserves the insertion geometry during tip guidance.

03 / Results and takeaways

Results and takeaways

Across five selected closed-loop trials, translation error decreased from roughly 55–129 mm to 0.08–2.51 mm. Four trials finished below 2 mm and maximum RCM residual stayed below 0.3 mm. Metrics are based on robot kinematics and configured tool geometry, not independent clinical measurements.

LAnD / 2026

Related research: LAnD and first contact

The co-authored manuscript “Visual Servoing of a Laparoscopic Ultrasound Probe without a Target Image” describes LAnD, combining surgeon aiming, IBVS, RCM-constrained control and force-based contact detection. Team experiments reached first contact less than 1 cm from the aimed point, with a peak force of 4.26 ± 0.38 N in the reported trials. These are experimental system results, not clinical validation.
